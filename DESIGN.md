# Design

## Audience and intent (written before generating anything)

**Who watches:** Ethiopian families, especially parents and grandparents who grew up with this tale, and Ethiopian diaspora followers who watch on phones. Secondary audience: colleagues in the 10 Academy cohort, who may not speak Amharic.

**Where:** a phone, vertical, scrolling a feed (TikTok, Facebook, Telegram), often with sound on low. The 16:9 cut is for YouTube and family TV.

**What they should feel or understand at the end:** recognition first ("I know this story, my grandmother told it"), then the moral landing as if for the first time: the patience, courage and gentleness Almaz used to tame a lion are the same things that will bring her husband back. No magic shortcut. Diaspora viewers should feel a small pull toward home.

**Choices that follow from that:**
- Amharic narration in a grandmother's voice, because that is how this story is heard at home. English subtitles under the Amharic so non-speakers and second-generation viewers follow every line.
- Hand-painted gouache look, not photoreal: it reads as a told story, avoids uncanny faces, and keeps the four characters consistent more easily.
- Tizita-mode krar and masinko score, slow: nostalgic and unmistakably Ethiopian.
- About 75 seconds, 11 shots: short enough to finish in a feed, long enough for the weeks of patience to feel like time passing (shots 6 and 7 are the same place, closer).
- The twist (shot 9, whiskers into the fire) gets a music stop so the surprise lands.

## Shot reasons

Every shot's reason is stored in `storyboard.json` (`shots[].reason`) so it travels with the shot. Summary: 1 place, 2 conflict, 3 her loneliness, 4 the healer, 5 the condition, 6 day one far away, 7 weeks later close, 8 the whiskers, 9 the twist, 10 the moral, 11 she begins again (mirrors shot 2).
